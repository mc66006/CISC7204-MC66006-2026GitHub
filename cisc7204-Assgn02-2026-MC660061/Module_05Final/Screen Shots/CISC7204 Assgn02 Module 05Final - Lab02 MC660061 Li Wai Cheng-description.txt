Assignment 02 — Module 05 Final, Lab 02: Automobile Sales Statistics Dashboard

The code-first sequence begins by importing Dash, pandas, Plotly Express, and graph objects, then reading the automobile-sales CSV. The dropdown_options list defines Yearly Statistics and Recession Period Statistics, while year_list provides the available years from 1980 through 2023. Screenshot 01 builds the page with an H1 title, a report-type Dropdown, a year Dropdown, and an output container. A small callback enables the year selector for the yearly report and disables it for the recession report, because the latter uses the full set of recession records rather than one chosen year.

The recession callback branch filters Recession == 1 and builds four separate summaries. groupby('Year') with mean feeds the sales-trend line; groupby('Vehicle_Type') with mean feeds the average-sales bar chart; grouping Advertising_Expenditure by Vehicle_Type and summing supplies the pie; grouping by unemployment_rate and Vehicle_Type with mean Automobile_Sales supplies the stacked comparison bar chart. The yearly branch filters the selected year for monthly and vehicle-specific views, while the annual trend uses all years. It sums Automobile_Sales by Month, reindexes month labels into Jan–Dec order, calculates vehicle-type average sales, and sums advertising expenditure by vehicle type. px.line, px.bar, and px.pie create the figures. Screenshots 02–04 show these calculation branches, the callback return structure, and app.run.

The rendered yearly report for 1980 uses a 2×2 layout: average sales by year, monthly total sales, vehicle-type average sales, and advertising expenditure share. The annual line has a conspicuous mid-1990s peak, while the 1980 monthly plot is correctly ordered from January to December and reaches its highest point in December. In that year, MediumFamilyCar has the largest vehicle-type average, and its advertising slice is also largest at 29.1%. The recession report switches to its four recession-specific charts and disables the year selector. Its vehicle-type bar is highest for SuperMiniCar, while SmallFamilyCar contributes the largest advertising share at 25.2%, followed by MediumFamilyCar at 24.4% and SuperMiniCar at 22.4%. The sales trend shows substantial variation across years, and the unemployment chart varies by both rate and vehicle class; it should be read as a comparison of observed averages, not evidence that unemployment alone caused the changes.
